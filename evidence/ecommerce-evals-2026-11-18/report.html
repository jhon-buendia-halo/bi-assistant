E-commerce Evals Report

100 new, harder questions over the Edge Cases store, each asked 3 times to Emily (one agent) and to the Answer a Business Question workflow. Every failure was read and sorted: real app errors, checks that rejected right answers, and questions open to another reading. The improvement plan comes from that sorting.

The questions

Written today with four AI helpers, 25 each, on the Edge Cases sample: a synthetic online store (300,000 orders, 1,000,000 order lines, 50,000 customers, shipments, payments, returns, reviews, a 3-level category tree) built with planted problems: the same value spelled several ways ("FedEx" and "FEDEX "), blanks next to NULLs, a legacy orders table with no key, an empty gift-card table, shipment dates before order dates, swapped promotion dates.

A · analytics

Multi-step joins up the category tree, rankings with ties, rates with a real denominator choice.

B · time and operations

Month and year comparisons, customer cohorts, delivery times, returns windows.

C · data traps

Business questions that need one planted problem handled to be right.

D · ask, refuse, chart

Ambiguous, out-of-scope and non-existent questions, charts, and five reworded pairs.

67 dev and 33 held-out, 15 categories. All 85 reference queries run and pass the repo's validator; the other 15 (ask, refuse, describe the data) are checked by behaviour. No person has reviewed the questions yet. File: backend/src/mastra/evals/sets/ecommerce.json

Improvement plan

In order. Each step names what it changes and the failures it should remove; the test after each is a re-run of the same 100 questions on both targets (about $1.70 and 45 minutes on gpt-5.6-luna).

  1. Fix the checks first done, re-run in section 2b

    Left open: record the workflow's clarifying question as an ask-tool call; one question to reword (ec-b-returns-lately); checks that fail an agent that recovered from its own query error; a few number checks that flag correctly derived totals.

    Make text checks ignore letter case; let the number check ignore dates and years; let the result check accept extra columns, other units (cents and dollars) and rounding; accept the workflow's own clarifying question for ambiguous questions; let the out-of-scope check allow supporting counts.

    Removes about 40 false failures for Emily and 33 for the workflow. Without it, every later score is wrong by 30 to 40 points.

  2. Tell the agents how this store's data is messy

    Add instructions to the Edge Cases dataset's ontology (the place the app already sends to every agent): spellings vary, so trim and compare in lower case; "delivered" and "returned" are order statuses; valid ratings are 1 to 5; ignore shipments and returns dated before their order; promotion dates are swapped; departments are the top of the category tree.

    Targets 14 wrong answers for Emily and 21 for the workflow. This is the most common real error, and it is the same work a client would do for their own data.

  3. Workflow: ask only when there is no sensible default

    A last rule for the plan step: when a question has one common reading, answer it and state the assumption; ask only when two readings give different answers and nothing in the question or the data picks one.

    Targets 14 to 15 workflow questions that ended on an unnecessary question.

  4. Emily: four rules

    Compare equal periods (the same months in both years); merge spellings instead of asking about them; always end with a final answer, even "not enough data"; describe the data from the catalog for "what do we have" questions, without queries.

    Targets 12 Emily failures, including the 4 runs with no answer.

  5. Workflow: charts and filters

    Confirm that the Build visual step runs and is recorded in evals (3 chart questions had no chart), and make the query step keep every filter the plan names and add none of its own.

    Targets 6 workflow failures.

  6. Review 11 questions with a person

    Questions where another reading is reasonable, such as returned orders by return rows or by status, units or lines for a return rate, UTC or store time for monthly counts. Decide the reading, then fix the question or its reference.

    best-subcategory-per-department, return-rate-by-department, returned-orders-payment-method, usd-average-order-value, cancelled-but-delivered-2025, recent-sales-trend, ytd-2026-vs-2025, quote-in-product-name, revenue-by-top-category, return-rate-2023 and its twin.

What this means for the decision

  • On harder questions over messy data, the single agent is more accurate than the workflow once the checks are fixed (59 against 41 right 3 of 3), and faster and cheaper. The same held on 25 easier questions earlier today (61% and 65% of attempts).
  • The workflow's step-by-step shape is not what fails it; its plan step's habit of asking back is, and that is one rule to change (P3).
  • Both agents fail mostly for the same reason: they do not know the data's quirks. That is a context problem the app already has a place for (P2), and it is what a client deployment will need too.
  • The numbers on this page are on gpt-5.6-luna. The beta ships on Claude, where these agents scored higher in earlier runs; the comparison should be repeated there.
